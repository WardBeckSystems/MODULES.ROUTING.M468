> 1982 WBS Frame restoration and customization
>
> At this point.. after removing the D.A. system.. i hadn't put any power in the console. 
>
> Still too much stuff to get used to before risking having smoke in there.. 
>
> (It seems that i really knew the whats/wheres/whys and whens but I had really good advice from very generous people here on the WBSPS)
>
> So.. Power Supplies... the back view of where the PSU (M625B) slide from the front. 2 power supplies. Giving +/- 25v D.C. needed to operate the audio signal of each input channels. 
>
> When I bought the console.. I only got the frame with its accessories.. but no input channels.. the sellers wanted to keep their modules (M460LA) and rack them. I only had 2x M460E that i bought a year ago in the project of racking them too. But since I just got a frame.. I would eventually be able to test lots of the things in the console with them. 
>
> So... Let the "beeping" start!! O.o
>
> MANY hours on beeping and figuring what is where regarding the pinout of each section. 
>
> As the Buss selector modules (M468K) were sitting on the top below the VU panels. I arguably knew that.. (at this point the PDF section of the WBSPS became my "day-to-day-go-to" IMMENSE THANK YOU to Anthony P. Kuzub for building and accumulating all these manuals and knitting them so we could all benefit from them. VERY long and exhausting work there. Again.. Thank you Tone.)... so... I knew that these modules were to route the signal from each input modules to the master section. In my case S1 to S6 and M1-M2. (S=Subs/M=Main/Master) I knew that below this section.. it had to be the input channels by how the modules (M460E) were designed physically and by how the slots appeared in the bucket inside the console. 
>
> After figuring what from the Input modules (M460E) was sitting on the patch bays, like; Mic input, Line input, inserts. Direct outs were not feeded to the patch bays. I eventually had to wire them. So after that.. the longest "beeping" passion went into the master section.. where all ancillaries were sitting. But since the frame didn't come with any master modules, Aux modules, Monitor modules, compressors or anything.. that was a bit mind boggling.

— **pawajari**, Nov 15, 2013|46

> 1982 WBS Frame restoration and customization
>
> SoooOOooooo.... 
>
> Now that everything is "mainly" figured out as per where the voltage sits on the connectors, what is sitting on the patch bay and getting familiar with the pinouts of each modules i had in the console, now is the time to get into the voltage section. 
>
> This implied MANY hours of prep. 
>
> Like making a test bench, understanding what was a test bench for, making sure the power supplies I had were in working order. M625B, M648, M624. Making testing jig for M460s and other misc modules. Etc, etc.. 
>
> Then.. What PSU does what? So yeah.. 
>
> M625B; +/- 25v D.C. audio signal alimentation
> M648; +48v D.C. for phantom power and future endeavors involving vintage 400 series modules. 
> M624; =24v D.C. for lighting and relays 
>
> So... during all of this time of beeping and making test bench, jigs, etc... I cleaned all the modules that were to be used in the console. 
>
> Like the Buss selector modules M468K and my faders.
>
> As for the Penny & Giles faders, i looked upon Google and read a lot on the WBSPS, there is an article regarding the cleaning of those faders. So information is out there. 
>
> Yeah.. i know.. we shouldn't be using vaseline as it has a sticky property and will eventually attract the dust and dirt. Shall I say i'll eventually have to start over. 
>
> As for the casings of the M468K.. i took some varsol and Mythil Hydrate to clean the rust and dirt out. Took some WD-40 for the switches and it came out nicely and operational.
